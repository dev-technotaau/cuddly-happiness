# Ring designer

A full-page configurator at `#/designer` where a visitor designs an engagement ring step by step. A real-time 3D view builds the ring as it is chosen; the visitor can turn it 360° at any moment and see it in a studio, in its box in the salon, or on a porcelain hand, under five lights. The price is itemised and updates with every choice. The atelier's notes and a budget finder guide the choices; the finished design goes to the bag, a design consultation, the try-on studio, a share, or the visitor's saved designs. It is a prototype: the stones and the 3D view illustrate the design, and nothing is ordered.

Scope: `src/pages/Designer.tsx`; the page's parts `src/components/designer/RingViewer.tsx`, `src/components/designer/Glyph.tsx`, `src/components/designer/LivePrice.tsx`, `src/components/designer/RingBreakdown.tsx`, `src/components/designer/DesignNotes.tsx`, `src/components/designer/CaratField.tsx`, `src/components/designer/BudgetFinder.tsx`, `src/components/designer/DesignSpec.tsx`, `src/components/designer/canDraw3d.ts`, `src/components/designer/three/gpu.ts`, `src/components/designer/three/accumulate.ts`, `src/components/designer/three/gemMaterial.ts`, `src/components/designer/three/raytrace.ts`, `src/components/designer/three/bvh.ts`, `src/components/designer/three/bvhWorker.ts`; the 3D engine `src/components/designer/three/stage.ts`, `src/components/designer/three/lighting.ts`, `src/components/designer/three/setups.ts`, `src/components/designer/three/ringModel.ts`, `src/components/designer/three/parts.ts`, `src/components/designer/three/gems.ts`, `src/components/designer/three/materials.ts`; `src/data/ring.ts`, `src/data/outline.ts`, `src/data/ringCopy.ts`, `src/data/ringPrice.ts`, `src/data/ringAdvice.ts`, the integration points in `src/store.tsx`, `src/App.tsx`, `src/components/lineDetails.ts`, `src/components/cart/CartLineItem.tsx`, `src/pages/Cart.tsx`, `src/pages/Checkout.tsx`, `src/components/contact/AskSheet.tsx`, `src/components/visit/VisitFlow.tsx`, `src/data/visits.ts`, `src/components/tryon/*`, `src/components/share/ShareSheet.tsx`

## 2. The model (`src/data/ring.ts`)

### 2.1 A design

`RingDesign` = `stone { type, shape, carat, colour, clarity, grade }`, `setting { style, height, cathedral, sides, sideCarat }`, `prongs { count: 4 | 6, double, tip, vTips, compass }`, `band { form, stones, coverage, width, taper }`, `metal { band, head, finish }`, `details { hiddenHalo, peekaboo, surprise, milgrain, engraving, font }`, `size: string | null`.

| Choice | Values |
|---|---|
| Stone | `diamond`, `sapphire`, `ruby`, `emerald` |
| Shape | `round`, `oval`, `cushion`, `emerald`, `radiant`, `princess`, `asscher`, `pear`, `marquise`, `heart` |
| Colour / clarity (diamonds) | `D`–`J` / `IF`, `VVS1`, `VVS2`, `VS1`, `VS2`, `SI1`, `SI2` |
| Grade (coloured stones) | `fine`, `extra`, `exceptional` |
| Setting | `solitaire`, `halo`, `three-stone`, `bezel`; height `low`, `classic`, `high`; cathedral shoulders; side stones `round`, `pear`, `baguette` |
| Claws | 4 or 6, double, tip `round`, `eagle` (“eagle claw”), `tab`; V-claws at a stone's points; compass claws |
| Band | form `classic`, `knife`, `split`, `twist`; stones `none`, `pave`, `channel`; coverage `half`, `three-quarter`, `full`; width; tapered |
| Metal | band `platinum`, `18k-white`, `18k-yellow`, `18k-rose`; head (the claws, or a bezel's rim) `match`, `platinum`, `18k-white`; finish `polished`, `satin`, `hammered` |
| Details | hidden halo, accent diamonds (`peekaboo`), surprise diamond, milgrain, engraving (18 characters, `ENGRAVING_MAX`) in `script`, `serif` or `block` |
| Ranges | `CARAT` 0.3–4 (0.01), `SIDE_CARAT` 0.1–0.75, `WIDTH` 1.6–3 mm (0.1), `MAGIC_CARATS` 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, `RING_SIZE_VALUES` 4–9 by halves |

`DEFAULT_DESIGN`: 1 ct round diamond, G VS1, classic-height solitaire, four round claws, classic 2.0 mm plain band, platinum, polished, no size. `DESIGN_SLUG` (`'bespoke-engagement-commission'`) is the catalog piece a designed ring rides on in the bag.

### 2.2 Measured: `layoutOf`

Everything the 3D view draws and the price counts comes from `layoutOf(d)` (millimetres): the centre stone's length, width and depth (`stoneSize`, from `CUTS[shape]` and the stone's specific gravity), the heights above the finger (band top, culet, girdle, table: `HEIGHT_GAP` low 0.25, classic 0.9, high 1.8, +0.6 with cathedral), the band (inner radius from `innerDiameter(size)`, size 6.5 when unsized), the claw angles (`prongAngles`, V-claws by `vTipAngles`), every small stone (halo, hidden halo, side stones, pavé or channel, accent diamonds, surprise), carat totals, the metal volume and grams per alloy (`DENSITY`), and the rise.

**Rings of small stones** (`MeleeRing`: `count`, `mm`, `carat`, `scale`, `offset`, `drop`, `runs`, `keep`, `hull`; 2026-10-24). A halo and a hidden halo are counted along their true paths (`outlinePath`: the centre stone's outline, a heart's hidden halo round its convex hull, scaled and grown), and the 3D view sets exactly those stones: each claw passes between two stones, kept clear by `clawClearance(wire, mm, apart)` along the path (its wire `clawWire`: finer with six claws, doubled, or inside a halo (× 0.82); `apart`, how far it stands toward the centre from the stones' line), and each free stretch takes a stone at each end, against its keep, and the rest between, `pitch` apart at least (`meleeRuns`, `meleePlaces` in `src/data/outline.ts`). The halo's stones are `clamp(width × 0.17, 1.05, 1.6)` mm, `HALO_GAP` 0.25 mm off the girdle, at least `mm + 0.06` apart: a 1.2 ct round takes 20, five between each two claws. The hidden halo's are `clamp(width × 0.13, 0.9, 1.25)` mm, leaning back `HIDDEN_TILT` (25°) round the pavilion, the top of each girdle 0.03 mm off it, low enough that the collar's outer edge stays inside the girdle seen from above (lower round a pear's point); where a stone wouldn't fit (beside a heart's cleft, round a point: its girdle or culet would come within 0.015 mm of the pavilion, or show past the girdle) its stretch is kept clear as a claw's is, tried only on cuts with points or a cleft where the path turns more than 20° across a stone. The rings are kept by what decides them (`keptRing`: the stone's measures and the claws; each cut's radius tables once, `radiiOf`), so the carat chart's and the options' many prices don't lay them out again (a cold layout with a hidden halo takes 1.5–4 ms). The surprise diamond is chosen to the band: `clamp(width × 0.55, 1, 1.6)` mm, `× 0.42` on a twist.

`normalize` keeps a design buildable: carats and widths clamped, side stones at most 0.6 × the centre; a twist or knife band can't take a channel (becomes pavé); six claws can't be doubled; a halo clears the hidden halo; a bezel clears the hidden halo and the accent diamonds; V-claws only on shapes with points; compass claws only on round, cushion, princess and Asscher (not a princess with V-claws); the engraving keeps only what can be engraved (`engravable`: letters, figures, spaces and `. , ' ’ & - – · : / + ! ? “ ” " « » ( ) ♥`, 18 at most); a size outside `RING_SIZE_VALUES` is cleared. `patch(d, p)` merges a `DesignPatch` and normalises.

### 2.3 The code

`encodeDesign(d)` writes `1` (the version) + each enum's index in base 36, then `-` flags (base 36 bits: cathedral, six claws, double, V-claws, compass, taper, hidden halo, accent diamonds, surprise, milgrain), the carat × 100, the side carat × 100, the width × 10, the size, and the engraving (`encodeURIComponent`). `decodeDesign(code: unknown)` returns `null` for anything that isn't a version-1 code, falls back to defaults for unknown values, and drops an engraving that won't decode. Example: `10133111110103000-e8-100-25-20-6-Toujours%20%C2%B7%202026`.

Two codes per design:

| Code | Built by | Carried by |
|---|---|---|
| The visitor's own | `encodeDesign(design)` | the address (`?r=`) and the draft, so a choice set aside (claws chosen before a bezel) comes back with the setting |
| The ring as it will be made | `designCode(d)` = `encodeDesign(canonical(d))` | the bag, visits, the try-on, “Ask a jeweller”, the share and saved designs |

`canonical(d)` sets back what doesn't apply to the ring as made: claws under a bezel, side stones without three stones, how far round on a plain band, the lettering when there are no words; the engraving is trimmed. Rings made the same therefore have one code, and stack in the bag. `isDesignCode(code)` is true only for a code that decodes and encodes back to itself; the store uses it on everything it reads back (`tns-cart`, `tns-designs`, `tns-design-draft`), so a cut-off or edited code is dropped rather than priced or shown differently.

The format is a contract once codes sit in bags: the option lists only ever grow at their end, and any other change takes a new version character.

### 2.4 House designs

`PRESETS`: `bonsecours`, `notre-dame`, `ville-marie`, `basilique`, `maisonneuve` (each with the `slug` of its catalog ring) and `sherbrooke` (“Sherbrooke Sapphire Halo” / “Halo saphir Sherbrooke”: a sapphire halo in yellow gold, no catalog piece). `surprise()` picks a shape, a setting and a band that suit each other.

## 3. Price (`src/data/ringPrice.ts`)

Cost-plus: `costsOf(d)` (CAD) counts the centre stone (diamonds: `PER_CARAT` steps × `COLOUR_K` × `CLARITY_K` × `SHAPE_K`; coloured stones: `GEM_PER_CARAT` by grade, +18% per carat above 1), side stones, each set of melee (`MELEE_PER_CARAT` 2200; accent diamonds and the surprise 2400; baguettes `BAGUETTE_PER_CARAT` 2300), band and head metal by grams × `METAL_RATE` (`src/data/costing.ts`, exported with `LABOUR_RATE_CAD` 95), CAD 280, bench hours × 95, setting labour per stone, finish, the report and shipping. The reports: the centre diamond's GIA report and, on a three-stone ring, one for each side diamond (`giaReport`: 165 from 0.50 ct, 95 below; the statement line “GIA grading reports · side stones” / “Two natural diamonds, graded and inscribed”), as the house says (`HOUSE.natural`); a coloured centre stone's gemmological report is 240. Melee (pavé, halo, accents) are priced as graded parcels, without reports of their own. `ringPrice(d, currency)` = the sum × `MARKUP` 1.34, to the nearest 10 CAD; USD through `toUSD`.

A designed ring is paid as the atelier works (owner decision, 2026-10-22: "whichever is best"): `DEPOSIT_SHARE` (30%) at checkout, `designDeposit(price)` = 30% of one ring's price to the nearest ten in the market's currency (never more than the price), which reserves its stones and starts the drawings and the wax; the balance is due when the visitor approves the final design, before casting (`HOUSE.deposit`). The bag, the cart page, checkout, the designer's review and the try-on all show the same deposit (see [../commerce-logic.md](../commerce-logic.md)).

`priceRing(d, currency, locale)` returns `{ lines, price, metalWeight, labourHours, layout }`: `CostLine`s in `materials` (the centre stone; “Side stones · round” for a three-stone pair; “Halo diamonds”, “Hidden halo diamonds”, “Pavé diamonds” or “Channel diamonds”, “Accent diamonds”, “Surprise diamond”; the metal, split into band and head when they differ), `making` and `services` (lines converted with `CAD_TO_USD`), included services (engraving, one resize in the first year unless the band is set all the way round, lifetime care), and `margin` (“Design, salon & maison margin”) = the price minus the rest, so the statement reconciles exactly. The house designs price within about −9% to +1% of their catalog rings (Ville-Marie 20,230 against 22,250 CAD).

## 6. The viewer (`RingViewer.tsx`)

Asks first whether the browser can draw in 3D (`canDraw3d`, free of three.js: WebGPU, or WebGL 2 for the renderer's fallback), loads the engine with `import('./three/stage')` (its own chunk), starts it (`Stage.create`, the renderer starting asynchronously) and builds the first ring inside `onStageClear`. The veil (“Preparing the 3D view…”) stays until the stage reports `onReady` (its shaders compiled). Without either it draws `Sketch` (the band and the stone's outline) with “This browser can’t show the ring in 3D. Every choice still applies, and the summary lists them all.”; a lost context offers “Show it again” (`btnQuiet`).

Controls: Studio / Salon / On the hand (`role="group"`), Light (presets with swatches, Brightness 0.5–1.6, Light angle, Fire 0–2, Reset), Views (Three-quarter, Front, Profile, From above, Inside the band), 360° (a slow turn; hidden with reduced motion), See its parts (exploded, with labels that follow the parts), Save a picture (1600 × 2000 JPEG drawn as an accumulated still, which takes a moment: the button pulses and is `aria-busy` meanwhile; hidden under `sm`), Full screen. The chips stack `gap-1.5` on phones so five fit the 260 px view. A panel closed from inside it (Escape, its ×, a view chosen) hands the focus back to its button; a press elsewhere just closes it. The canvas is focusable: arrows turn it, + and − zoom. The wheel zooms only with Ctrl or ⌘ (a trackpad pinch) or in full screen, so the page still scrolls over it. The hint (“Drag to turn · Ctrl + scroll to zoom”) is espresso at 70% on the light rooms, `white/60` in the salon, and steps aside while a change's caption shows. The page's handle (`ViewerApi`): `snapshot(kind, w, h)` (a promise: every picture is an accumulated still), `turnAround()`, `focus(on)`. Design changes reach the stage at most once a frame; a step change (or an engraving appearing) moves the camera.

## 7. The 3D engine (`src/components/designer/three/`)

| Module | What it does |
|---|---|
| `stage.ts` `Stage` | Started with `Stage.create` (the renderer from `gpu.ts`; sRGB, `NeutralToneMapping`, pixel ratio ≤ 2 on WebGPU and 1.5 on WebGL 2, lowered by 0.25 steps when frames run slow), a long lens (`PerspectiveCamera` fov 15, 19 on tall stages, near 2: about 90 mm on a full frame, as rings are photographed; the framings, set for 28°, are drawn from `LENS` × further away, so the near side of the band no longer swells against the head), `OrbitControls` (damped, no pan, distance and polar limits from the setup). Two `RenderPipeline`s: **moving**, the scene pass with motion vectors and the tracing pass's outputs (`mrt({ output, velocity, rtNormal, rtSpec })`) → the ring's shadow on what it rests in or on (`RingTracer.shade` in an `rtt` pass at half the size, read unfiltered, smoothed by `soften`) → the metals' reflections traced and that shadow laid on (`RingTracer.reflect` in an `rtt` pass, its quad turned with the room) → TRAA (temporal reprojection anti-aliasing, which jitters the camera itself) → bloom (strength 0.07 + 0.1 × fire, radius 0.22, threshold 2.4: only sparks); **still**, the `Accumulator`'s average → the same bloom, each sample drawn through a `TracedFrame` with a lens (depth of field: an aperture of 0.006 × the distance in focus, focused where the camera looks). While anything moves (`wake`: controls, glides, assembly, a new light) it draws moving frames; once at rest it settles: the still is seeded with TRAA's last frame (worth 6 samples), then one jittered sample a frame with the stones in single wavelengths, up to 128 samples (64 on a phone; 64/32 on WebGL 2), and it stops drawing. Pauses off screen, in hidden tabs, and while compiling or drawing a picture (`paused`). The first build is held and compiled against both targets the scene is drawn into (the moving pass and the still's frame), then played as the camera comes in. **Tracing** (`syncTracer`, each frame): any change of shape (`RingModel.version`) stops the tracing at once; once the parts have settled (`busyUntil`), the ring's triangles (`traceable`) are handed to the tracer, whose hierarchy is built off the page's thread, and the traced light fades in (0.4 s); then the floor's shadow is baked again (`GroundShadow`, a batch a frame in the background, double-buffered; the still starts again over the new floor), as it is when the light or the setup changes; the room's lights are first placed about the ring (`setLights`), for the floor's shadow and the salon's and the hand's. ## Notes & gotchas

- Everything is illustrative: the stones are drawn from their cut and grade, prices are indicative, and the shapes are the atelier's working proportions. The review says so.
- Checkout takes a designed ring's 30% deposit; the balance waits for the approval of the final design (owner decision, 2026-10-22). In Shopify this is a deposit selling plan (`shopify-integration.md` B9).
- Every diamond comes with its GIA report (owner decision, 2026-10-22): `HOUSE.natural`, the announcement bar, Home's services, the piece page's FAQ and the bag's perks say so, and the designer prices a report for the centre diamond and a three-stone ring's side diamonds. Pavé and halo stones are not graded one by one by GIA in practice; if the house wants the claim literally true of every small stone, its wording is for the owner (todo.md).
- The bloom threshold must stay above the brightest backdrop (linear ~0.9): at 0.86 the whole studio glowed and the ring washed out.
- `three`'s `computeFrenetFrames` picks an arbitrary normal; flat claws pass `wire(…, side)` or they turn edge-on.
- Gem shaders compile slowly (seconds on some GPUs); that is why the first build is held and compiled ahead. Later shapes reuse the program (the plane array is a fixed 128, the count a uniform). `compileAsync` compiles for the renderer's current target and MRT, so the stage points it at the moving pass (with its motion vectors) and then at the still's sample, with the live loop paused meanwhile: a frame drawn while the target is pointed elsewhere builds the wrong pipeline.
- Opaque node materials (not transparent, normal blending) force alpha to 1: the accumulator's passes and the try-on's TRAA resolve are unblended (`NoBlending`) so a transparent ground stays transparent. `RendererUtils.resetRendererState` sets an opaque black clear colour; the accumulator saves the state instead.
- The stills follow the same deterministic path as the moving view (out at the first facet that lets it), so the stone sharpens as it settles instead of changing character; only the wavelength and the sub-pixel position vary from sample to sample.
- The film grain is left off the designer's page (`Grain` in `App.tsx`): over a 3D view it reads as noise.
- An `IntersectionObserver` only reports crossings: a zero-height sentinel jumped over (an instant scroll to the top under reduced motion) is never seen. The phone bar's observer therefore extends its root far above the screen, so “the end has come up” is a state, not a crossing.
- The band and several claws and arches used to be wound inward: the GPU drew each part's far inside walls instead of its near outside, a hollow-face illusion in which the near and far sides of the ring read the wrong way round (the owner's "illusion", 2026-10-23). `outward` turns every closed sweep by its signed volume; the metals' tracing relies on it (a ray's start is lifted along the surface's own normal, which must point out of the metal).
- The metals are traced in a pass after the scene, not in their own shaders: with the walk compiled into a metal's shader, every metal pixel cost about three times as much even where nothing was traced (45 against 15 ms a still sample on the Iris Xe, the shader's registers, not the work). The pass swaps `rtSpec` × (traced − the room along the same mirror direction), so the direction must be three's own (`pow4(roughness)` mixed toward the normal).
- A still traces the mirror direction too (two turns, one while moving): sampling a polished metal's gloss only added noise where a reflection skims the band.
- TSL statements (`If`, `Loop`) are emitted only inside a `Fn` or a material's own setup; called at the top level, they silently vanish (a walk "that never hits").
- A pass draws without a scene: environment lookups in it turn with the pass material's own `envMapRotation`, so the stage gives the `rtt` pass's quad (three 0.186.1's `_quadMesh`) and `TracedFrame`'s the scene's `environmentRotation`. The floor's bake and the setups' shadow aim at the room's lights instead (`roomLights` places them as turned; `setLights` about the ring, as far from it as from the room's centre, so they lie where its reflections show them: on the hand the ring is some 150 mm above the room's centre).
- Dense hierarchies: the milgrain (hundreds of grains) stays out of the tracing; traced, it doubled the hierarchy for nothing a reflection would show. A seated frame (some 80,000 faces drawn) is traced as its coarser copy, the prongs as theirs (a seventh of the faces). Tracing the light leaving the melee (2026-10-24) costs a fifth of a still's sampling rate on a halo with pavé (33 against 42 samples a second on the Iris Xe) and nothing measurable while the ring turns (34 fps either way).
- A glancing reflection carries far: a mirror face whose triangles are broad and flat starts each reflection a little off the true surface, and a reflection skimming the band carries that into steps. The surprise diamond's first insert, joined to the band's coarse grid (0.25 × 0.5 mm) by long thin triangles, broke the band's reflection of itself into a staircase there; exact normals, finer angles, depth and normal precision changed nothing. The insert is rings of small faces on the inside's exact surface.
- A seated frame's traced copy must never stand proud of the drawn frame: a reflection leaving the drawn surface would start inside the traced metal and meet it at once. Its seats are cut wider by half its cell's diagonal (`erode`), more than a coarse face ever rises over the cut it spans.
- The halo's stones are placed on its 3D path, stepped round any V-claws, with the layout's runs: there the path is a little longer than the layout's, and the stones spread a touch wider. The hidden halo's keeps are the layout's own (`L.hiddenHalo.keep`), on the very same path.
- A picture asked for while another was being drawn used to come back empty: "View on you", pressed while the review's card was drawn (1.6 s after the review opens), did nothing. `snapshot` now queues.
- The headless checks use Chrome with the GPU (WebGPU on an Intel Iris Xe; a halo with pavé turns at 34 fps after the settings of 2026-10-24: after the tracer, 44 fps turning a solitaire at 1440 × 900 and 31 fps a halo with pavé before the resolution adapts, 60 at a phone's size; a still sample 19 and 29 ms; with the setups' traced shadow, 52 fps turning in the box and 47 on the hand against 53 and 54 without it, a still sample 25–29 ms there; `?gl=webgl` checks the WebGL 2 fallback). ANGLE's shader compiler logs precision warnings (`X4122`), which are harmless.
- Read everything a pass needs from a texture once, at its top, before any `If` or `Loop`: a texture read with its own filtering (implicit derivatives) inside a branch or a loop is undefined. One such read lost the receivers' normal's z and over-darkened their shadow up to twofold; inside a loop, read texels' centres at level 0.
- An expression handed to several sibling branches (`If` after `If`, as when a corner is kept into one of five slots) must be held in a variable first: TSL emits it in the first branch that uses it and the others silently read nothing (the horizon's crossing in `facing` came out as zero until it was).
- A running average in half floats darkens: rounding a fraction of the last bit down at every sample, a 128-sample still came out 1–2% darker than its samples (a uniform 0.5 averaged to 0.491). The average is kept in 32-bit floats (`averageType`); its targets are unfiltered, as a 32-bit float texture may not filter.
- The receivers' shading was checked against brute force (every triangle of the ring on the CPU, at the same surface points, with three's own normals): the glow, the lights and both together agree within the references' noise (±0.007). While moving, a single ray a frame, clamped at 0 where a heavily blocked light's sample counts for more than the light gives, reads up to 2% lighter in a deep shadow; the still is exact.
- The engraving's faces must be valid CSS `font` shorthand, the style and weight before the size (`italic 500 111px 'Baskervville', serif`): written after the size, the browser drops the whole string and draws in 10 px sans-serif (the serif and block lettering did until 2026-10-23).
- A split shank is drawn as two strands that meet at the bottom: an engraving there crosses the seam between them.
- In development the stage is exposed as `window.__ring` (DEV builds only) for the scratch tests.
- `CartUpsell` treats a designed ring as a `custom` piece (it is the commission underneath).
- “Start afresh” used to only swap the ring for the classic solitaire, and `commit` ignores a design that is already the current one: on a later step it seemed to do little, and in the “Welcome back” note over a draft that was only the classic solitaire (drafted by a visit that changed nothing) it did nothing at all, the note staying. Fixed 2026-10-24: no note over such a draft, and `startAfresh` resets the steps too.
- A pinned (sticky) element can't be scrolled to: it never leaves the screen, so a test of whether it has scrolled out of sight is never true, and scrolling to it goes nowhere. On a desktop the price-and-steps block is pinned under the header, and a step change measured it: after scrolling down a step, "Next" left the page where it was (fixed 2026-10-24 by measuring the step's own section).
