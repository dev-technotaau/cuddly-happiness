# Home: the final refine

Status: **approved and built** (2026-10-21). The owner's answers: hero keeps four slides; the making-of moves to the Atelier; Our promise keeps six tiles; Worn in Montréal stays; the gift finder moves to the Gifts chapter. Measured after: 17.9 desktop screens (from 31.5), 23.8 phone screens (from 41), 14 blocks (from 25), 1,147 words (from 1,830), 9 booking buttons (from 15). On the Watches chapter the spotlight opens without its list of watches, which the grid below already shows.

The legacy redesign added to Home without removing anything, as asked at the time. The result does every job, but too many times, and now works against the brief: *calm and luxurious, lots of white space, large photos, few words; neither bored nor overwhelmed; everything on purpose*. This plan halves Home and keeps every feature: most sections that leave Home already live on a deeper page, and the rest move to one.

## 1. Where Home stands (measured today)

First visit, English, measured in the browser.

| | Today |
|---|---|
| Blocks on the page | 25 (18 numbered acts plus 7 bands) |
| Length, desktop 1440 × 900 | 28,316 px = **31.5 screens** |
| Length, phone 390 × 844 | 34,734 px = **41 screens** |
| Words | 1,830 |
| Links and buttons | 145 |
| Booking buttons ("Book a visit / a fitting / a consultation") | 15 |

Why that matters:

- **Most attention goes to the first screens.** In Nielsen Norman Group's eyetracking research (2018), people spent 57% of their viewing time above the fold and 74% within the first two screenfuls. A long page isn't wrong in itself, but every extra screen dilutes the few that are actually seen.
- **Home repeats other pages.** Four Home sections are exact repeats of other pages: the Four Cs, the tennis guide, the films and "The hands".
- **Too many booking buttons.** With 15 of them, "Book a visit" stops feeling like a privilege and starts feeling like a pitch.

## 2. The test every section must pass

1. **One job.** Each section does one of Home's five jobs:
   - make the house felt (legacy and trust);
   - show the two signatures;
   - show rent or own;
   - prove the in-house making;
   - open the doors: look at pieces, book a visit, ask a jeweller.
2. **Best version on Home.** It's the best version of that job on Home, not a second one.
3. **No better page.** No deeper page does it better. If one does, Home links there instead.

## 5. Polish on what stays

- **Few words.** Every act's body copy is at most two sentences; headings stay. Both languages.
- **One door per act.** Booking buttons go from 15 to about 8: the hero, the close of act 01, "Book a fitting" in 04, "Book a design consultation" in 05, act 09 and the salon. "Ask a jeweller" stays in act 09, the salon and the footer.
- **Smoother scroll.** The pinned scroll leaves Home, so the page scrolls in one continuous glide with nothing to re-measure.
- **Lighter page.** Home no longer autoplays the films or loads the try-on and making-of imagery, so the first load is lighter.
- **Numbering** re-flows by itself (the `ACTS` list), so the acts read 01–10.
